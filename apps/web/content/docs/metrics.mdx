# Metrics

Five measurements ship in `@cupel/core/src/metrics`. This page gives each one its proper name,
states what it computes, why it exists, and where it is blind. The landing page refers to them
in plain words; the mapping is noted per metric.

Every claim below is pinned by the core test suite. Where a limit or regime is described, there
is a test that exercises it.

## SSIM: windowed grayscale structural similarity

Landing page name: structural survival.

`ssim(a, b)` computes structural similarity over 8x8 windows on the Rec. 601 grayscale plane of
two images with identical dimensions, returning a single score where 1 means structurally
identical.

Contracts held by tests:

- An image against itself scores exactly 1.0, not 0.9999999.
- The measure is symmetric: `ssim(a, b) === ssim(b, a)`.
- Against progressively stronger blur, the score decreases monotonically.

Blind spot: SSIM here is computed on grayscale. A change that moves colour while preserving
luma, which is precisely what aggressive chroma subsampling does, can leave SSIM at 1.0. That
blindness is not fixed inside SSIM; it is covered by the next metric.

## CIE76 deltaE: perceptual colour distance

Landing page name: colour drift.

`deltaE(a, b)` converts both images from sRGB through linear RGB and XYZ (D65 white point) to
Lab, computes the CIE76 distance per pixel, and returns both the mean and the 95th percentile.
The p95 matters because localized colour damage disappears into a mean.

The justification test, kept explicit in the suite because it is the entire reason the metric
exists: construct two images whose Rec. 601 luma is equal pixel for pixel but whose hue differs.
Grayscale SSIM scores the pair above 0.999; deltaE reports a large distance. Roughly 2.3 CIE76
units is the conventional just noticeable difference.

## Tiled Laplacian sharpness

Landing page name: detail.

`laplacianSharpness(img)` normalizes the image so its long edge is at most 1024 (never
upscaling), converts to grayscale, applies a 3x3 Laplacian, and computes the response variance
per 64-pixel tile. It reports the 95th percentile across tiles rather than the mean: one sharp
region proves the source is sharp even when most of the frame is bokeh. Edge slivers thinner
than 16 pixels are skipped as statistically untrustworthy.

Comparability regime, stated plainly: because normalization never upscales, p95 values are
strictly comparable only among images whose long edge is at least 1024. Smaller images are
measured at native scale, where absolute numbers run higher. Detecting that a large image was
upscaled is not this metric's job; that belongs to effective resolution below.

## Blocking: 8x8 boundary energy ratio

Landing page name: seam energy.

`blockingScore(img)` measures mean absolute luma gradient at pixel positions falling on 8x8
block boundaries and divides it by the same measure at interior positions, separately for
horizontal and vertical, plus a combined score. A ratio meaningfully above 1.0 indicates
block-based compression in the file's history.

The important use case is laundered files: a JPEG re-saved as PNG or lossless WebP keeps its 8x8
seams. The container says lossless; the seams say otherwise, and the bytes are being spent on
damage. Degenerate inputs are defined, not accidental: a perfectly flat image carries no
evidence either way and scores exactly 1.0 (neutral), and axes shorter than 17 pixels are
reported as neutral because the boundary/interior split is not meaningful there.

## Radially averaged power spectrum and effective resolution

Landing page name: real resolution.

`radialPowerSpectrum(img)` computes the FFT power spectrum on a square analysis plane (256, 512,
or 1024, adapted to the image's long edge) and averages it radially. `effectiveResolution(img)`
finds where spectral energy drops below a noise floor and converts that cutoff into an implied
pixel dimension.

A native image has a cutoff ratio near 1.0. An image upscaled 2x rolls off near 0.5: the extra
pixels carry no new frequency content, and the test suite pins that a 2x-upscaled image measures
approximately half its declared dimensions.

Read the result carefully, because the type is designed to prevent a specific misreading. For
images larger than the analysis plane, the measurement can only certify detail up to the plane
size, reported as `certifiedUpTo`. When `effective` equals `certifiedUpTo` and both are below
`declared`, the image carries at least that much real detail and possibly more. It must not be
read as upscaled.

## How the five compose

No single number is trusted alone, because each has a documented blind spot: SSIM misses colour,
deltaE says nothing about sharpness, Laplacian variance cannot see upscaling above its
normalized scale, blocking is silent on blur, and the spectrum is silent on colour. Together
they cover each other, which is why the provenance layer (M3 on the [roadmap](/docs/roadmap))
consumes all five when it assembles its evidence, and why the landing page demonstrates each one
against a specimen designed to fool the others.
